The price of tapping “accept”: 25 billion dollars

The promise you got when you tapped “accept” on Facebook now has a price tag.

In September, a jury in Santa Fe found that Meta had misled users in New Mexico, among other things about who could access their data. The jury found 26 of 29 statements misleading. On 21 October, Judge Francis Mathew set the penalty at 25 billion dollars.

The case began after the Cambridge Analytica scandal, when data from up to 87 million Facebook users was harvested through a third-party app. State law allows up to 5,000 dollars per violation, and the jury counted over 43 million. The money goes to New Mexico’s schools.

Meta says it does not sell user data and plans to appeal. In 2019, the US Federal Trade Commission fined Meta 5 billion dollars. This penalty is five times larger, from a single state.

When broken privacy promises cost billions, perhaps they will be kept. What have the apps on your phone promised you?

YOUR VALUE

What were you worth to Meta last year?

About 57 dollars. That is Meta’s own figure for revenue per daily user across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger in 2025. Alone, you are worth a dinner out. Together, the users are the whole business model. What if we negotiated as a group?

HEALTH DATA

Samsung asked for your pulse. Saying no came with a threat

In July, Samsung Health asked for consent to train AI on sleep, heart rate and medical data. If you said no, a warning said your health data would be deleted. After protests, Samsung backed down. A yes under threat is not a real choice.

DNA

Your DNA can end up in a bankruptcy

When 23andMe went bankrupt in 2025, customers’ genetic data and spit samples were sold to a nonprofit led by the company’s own cofounder. California objected to the sale but lost in court. Now the state has agreed a 6 million dollar settlement over a 2023 data breach that hit nearly 7 million customers. A court must still approve it. Customers can still ask to have their data deleted.
